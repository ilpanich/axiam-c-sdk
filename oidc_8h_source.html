1/*
2 * AXIAM C SDK — OIDC relying party, logout, device grant and token exchange
3 * (CONTRACT.md §12, §12.7, §14, §15).
4 *
5 * WHY THIS FILE EXISTS AT ALL. Contract 1.10 and earlier deferred §12 in the
6 * Swift, C and C++ SDKs: these are device- and IoT-oriented, and the
7 * browser-redirect relying-party flow has no natural home in any of them.
8 * Contract 1.11 (§12.6) reverses that, and the reason is worth keeping next to
9 * the code. The persona argument only ever covered two of the thirteen
10 * operations —
11 * `oidc_begin` and `oidc_exchange`, the pair that genuinely assumes a browser.
12 * The other seven are exactly what an embedded consumer wants:
13 * `login_client_credentials` is machine-to-machine login, `introspect` and
14 * `revoke` are ordinary questions a device asks about its own credentials, and
15 * `oidc_refresh` is the grant the §9 single-flight guard was built for.
16 * Meanwhile §14 (device grant) exists *because* a device cannot show a browser,
17 * and §20 (UMA) already gave this SDK a `/oauth2/token` call — so by 1.10 it was
18 * speaking OAuth2 at the token endpoint anyway, without the shared discovery
19 * cache and ID-token validation §12 specifies. This file removes a divergence
20 * rather than adding one.
21 *
22 * THE FOUR THINGS THIS SURFACE WILL NOT DO FOR YOU:
23 *
24 * 1. It stores no `state`, `nonce` or `code_verifier` (§12.3 rule 1). They come
25 * back out of axiam_oidc_begin() and the caller hands them to
26 * axiam_oidc_exchange(). There is no implicit cache, and the caller must
27 * also remember its own `redirect_uri` — RFC 6749 §4.1.3 requires it
28 * replayed byte-identically and axiam_authorization_request_t deliberately
29 * does not carry it (§12.1).
30 * 2. It never skips ID-token validation. There is no flag, no "insecure" entry
31 * point, and no partial result: §12.4 rule 7 is all-or-nothing, so a token
32 * set whose `id_token` fails any check is discarded whole — the access and
33 * refresh tokens from the same response never reach the caller.
34 * 3. It adopts nothing. Every operation here returns tokens; none of them
35 * become this client's own credential. §15.2 rule 5 makes that a MUST NOT
36 * for the exchanged token specifically, and this SDK takes the same posture
37 * everywhere rather than having two.
38 * 4. It does not retry a grant. §16.2 lists `oidc_exchange`, `device_authorize`,
39 * `device_login` and `token_exchange` as ineligible, because their
40 * credentials are single-use: retrying replays a spent authorization code or
41 * device code and turns a blip into a hard `invalid_grant`. Only the
42 * read-only calls here — discovery, introspection, a `device_poll` that hit
43 * a 5xx — go through §16.
44 *
45 * OWNERSHIP. Every out-parameter carrying heap memory has a matching _dispose,
46 * and every one is safe on NULL and on a zeroed struct, so a caller can dispose
47 * unconditionally on the error path.
48 */
