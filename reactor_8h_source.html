1/*
2 * AXIAM C SDK — Reactor (CONTRACT.md §22), the protocol core over a
3 * caller-supplied transport.
4 *
5 * WHAT THIS SHIPS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT.
6 *
7 * §22.1–§22.8 and §22.14 in full: the §8 v2 verification set on the event, the
8 * canonical serialization and MAC in both directions, the §22.5 registry and its
9 * mutable-field allow-lists, §22.8's strictest-wins default, and the declarative
10 * binding table. What it does NOT do is open a connection. §22.11 defers only
11 * the transport, because there is no maintained AMQP client for the targets this
12 * SDK serves that this project is willing to vendor.
13 *
14 * That split is the newer one, and it is worth stating. Until contract 1.28 this
15 * SDK shipped nothing from §22 while the section still bound an integrator to
16 * §22.1–§22.8 — so the half deferred for want of a *dependency* was the
17 * transport, and the half left to hand-roll from prose was the **protocol**: v2
18 * HMAC over a canonical serialization with a `null` signature placeholder,
19 * freshness in both directions, nonce and correlation binding, the allow-lists.
20 * That is the half with the sharp edges, none of them AMQP-shaped, and asking
21 * every integrator to reimplement it is how a signing bug ships.
22 *
23 * THE TRANSPORT SEAM HAS EXACTLY TWO CAPABILITIES (§22.11 rule 1): take the next
24 * delivery, and publish a reply to a named destination. It is not wider than that
25 * on purpose — a seam that also exposed declare, bind or queue-name derivation
26 * would hand an integrator the tools §22.1 forbids using.
27 */
